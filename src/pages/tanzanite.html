The story of Tanzanite

A gemstone found in only one place on Earth — and the heart of everything we do.

A Once-in-a-Generation Gem

Born beneath Kilimanjaro

Tanzanite was discovered in 1967 in the Merelani Hills of northern Tanzania, a few kilometres from the foothills of Mount Kilimanjaro. To this day, this small area remains the only known source of the gemstone in the world.

Tanzanite is the blue-violet variety of the mineral zoisite. Its signature colour comes from its unusual optical character: viewed from different angles, a single crystal can show blue, violet and even burgundy tones. Skilled cutters orient each stone to bring out its richest colour.

Because supply comes from a single location, every Tanzanite carries a piece of Tanzania with it — which is why we are proud to call ourselves the home of Tanzanite.

Buyer's Guide

How Tanzanite is graded

Like all fine gemstones, Tanzanite is judged on four qualities. Our experts will walk you through each one in person.

01

Colour

The most important factor. The finest stones show a deep, saturated violet-blue; lighter tones offer a softer, more delicate look.

02

Clarity

Most fine Tanzanite is "eye clean" — free of inclusions visible to the naked eye — which lets its colour shine.

03

Cut

A good cut balances colour, brilliance and weight. Popular shapes include oval, cushion, pear, emerald and trillion.

04

Carat

Colour tends to look richer in larger stones, so carat weight and colour are best considered together.

Caring for Your Tanzanite

Made to be worn for a lifetime

  • Clean gently with warm, soapy water and a soft brush.
  • Avoid ultrasonic and steam cleaners.
  • Protect it from hard knocks and sudden temperature changes.
  • Store it separately from harder gems to prevent scratches.
  • Remove rings during heavy work, sport or gardening.
